Why it exists

A lab, not a demo

Real payment systems fail in boring, expensive ways: a message gets lost, a customer is charged twice, fraud slips through quietly. QueueDesk is a working miniature of such a system where you can trigger each failure on demand and watch the safety nets catch it.

  • Real parts, toy money: an actual database, an actual message queue — but every dollar is pretend
  • Honest status: if something is unresolved, the screen says “pending” — it never fakes “all good”
  • 12 disaster drills: unplug the queue, crash mid-payment, send poison — each with a saved report card
  • A live AI guard: a real fraud-checking AI screens payments under a strict $1 spending cap

Architecture

Four parts, one laptop

Everything runs on this machine — nothing in the cloud except the AI fraud-checker.

1 · The engine (takes payments)

  • A web service receives payments and hands them to the queue
  • The database remembers everything: payments, delivery notes, fraud checks, history
  • The message queue delivers each payment at least once; the worker ignores repeats

2 · The dashboard (watch only)

  • A web UI shows payments, fraud holds, queue depths, and traces
  • Strictly read-only — looking can never change or break anything
  • Unknowns show as dashes; “pending” means pending, never guessed

3 · The security desk

  • Simple rules plus an AI adviser screen every payment before money moves
  • Every decision and its reasons are stored for review

4 · The drill instructors

  • A traffic generator sends scripted payments — normal ones and fraud attempts
  • A verifier runs 12 disaster drills and grades each with a report card

Risk gate

Rules decide. AI advises.

Three simple tripwires catch the obvious stuff; an AI adviser double-checks. And a strict rule: the AI can never approve, block, or accuse anyone — it only answers two yes/no questions as background.

The three tripwires

  • Stranger danger — first-ever payment to this person? → hold for review
  • Weird amount — $87,000 when you usually send $500? → hold for review
  • Hard limit — $95,000+ to a stranger? → blocked
  • If the AI can't be reached, rules still apply; quiet payments → held safely

Four strictness settings

  • Off — everything sails through (for testing the plumbing)
  • Rules only — tripwires without the AI
  • Rules + pretend AI — a free built-in stand-in, same questions
  • Rules + real AI — the live model, under a $1 spending cap

Live AI · $1 cap

The AI guard, on a prepaid meter

“Jev” is an outside AI service that answers two questions per payment: is the recipient new? and is the amount unusual? It sees only numbers — never names, never account details.

  • Prepaid $1 meter: every answer costs a fraction of a cent; the meter lives in the database, so restarting the app can't reset it
  • Hard stop at $1: once the dollar is spent, checks pause and payments queue for review — shown on screen, never silent
  • Observed cost: one real check took 0.3 seconds and cost $0.00002 — two-thousandths of a cent
  • It already taught us something: the AI reports “confidence” differently than our test double did — found on the first live call, fixed and locked in with a test

Failure lab

Twelve disaster drills — on purpose

Each drill breaks one thing, then checks the safety net held. A few favorites:

“Unplug it mid-flight”

  • Kill the message queue — payments pile up safely, drain cleanly after restart
  • Crash halfway through saving — redelivery produces exactly one result, never two
  • Send a poison message — quarantined with a reason; everything else keeps flowing

“Confuse it”

  • Click “pay” twice — charged once; change the amount mid-retry — rejected loudly
  • Lose the bank's reply — chased up and settled; truly unknown stays “pending”
  • Flood it / starve it — bursts absorb, deadlines admit only what fits

One command runs every drill plus all unit tests and grades the report card: pass, or exactly what failed.

Dashboard

Read-only operations view

A control tower for the lab: six screens, every number traceable to a real reading. Nothing here can change anything — it's windows, not levers.

The three you'll use most

  • Overview — the big picture: how many paid, how many held, queue health
  • Payments — the ledger, newest first; filter by experiment run
  • Risk review — the held-payments queue with reasons, plus the AI spend meter

For digging deeper

  • Queues / Ops — is the conveyor belt backed up? any guards tripped?
  • Telemetry / Scenarios — follow one payment's trail; replay past drills
  • Missing data shows as a dash — the screen would rather admit ignorance than invent a number

Operate

Try it: four commands

1 · Start everything
./start-lab

Launches the database, queue, engine, and dashboard. Safe to re-run; won't fight for busy ports.

2 · Send 20 payments, fraud mixed in
./scripts/generate --run-id w1 --count 20 --scenario risk-2 --send --observe

Shows the plan first without sending; --send fires it, --observe watches each one settle.

3 · Clean slate · 4 · Prove health
./scripts/reset-lab  &  ./scripts/verify

Wipe the pretend data between experiments; run every test and all 12 disaster drills.
